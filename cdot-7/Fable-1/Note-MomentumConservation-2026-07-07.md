## 1. The Premise, Inverted

It is Milgrom's **original 1983 MOND** — the algebraic relation
$\mu(g/a_0)\,g=g_N$ imposed pointwise, or per-particle modified inertia — that
violates momentum conservation: two unequal masses experience unequal-and-opposite
mutual forces, so an isolated binary self-accelerates. **AQUAL (Bekenstein &
Milgrom 1984) was constructed precisely to repair this**: as a Lagrangian field
theory,
$$\mathcal L=-\frac{a_0^2}{8\pi G}\,\mathcal F\!\left(\frac{|\nabla\Phi|^2}{a_0^2}\right)
-\rho\,\Phi+\tfrac12\rho v^2,$$
its spatial translation invariance guarantees (Noether) exact conservation of the
total momentum of matter + field, and Bekenstein–Milgrom proved the total
self-force on an isolated system vanishes. This is why premise 4 specifies
"AQUAL-spirit" — the conservation laws were the selling point, not a casualty.

**Likely source of the confusion:** in AQUAL, *pairwise* Newton's third law fails
(the field equation is nonlinear; superposition does not hold), so
$\vec F_{A\to B}\neq-\vec F_{B\to A}$ body-by-body even though the field-inclusive
total momentum is exactly conserved. Compressions of the former into "AQUAL does
not conserve momentum" appear occasionally and are wrong as stated.

## 2. Why the cdot Modifications Do Not Spoil It

The framework adds two time dependences to the local Lagrangian: $a_0(t)=
\lambda\dot c(t)$ and $m(t)\propto c^{1/2}$. Both enter as **spatially uniform**
parameters (premise 1's homogeneity), so:
- spatial translation and rotation invariance are intact $\Rightarrow$ momentum and
  angular momentum conserved **exactly**, at every epoch;
- time-translation invariance is broken $\Rightarrow$ energy non-conserved at
  $O(H)$ — the familiar situation of any time-dependent background, already
  recorded for the photon sector.

Also worth recording: the cosmological closure never invokes the field theory's
non-spherical structure — it uses only the exact spherical relation
$\mu(g_h/a_0)g_h=GM_h/R_h^2$, on which AQUAL, QUMOND, and the algebraic law all
agree. The AQUAL commitment matters (and its conservation laws with it) only in the
local, non-spherical sector — exactly where it was invoked.

## 3. Momentum Conservation Is Load-Bearing, Not Incidental

Taking the question seriously exposes a unification the framework had not stated:
**all of the framework's "redshift" phenomenology is momentum conservation read
against growing local units.**

1. **Photons:** conserved momentum is $\hbar k$ — the conserved-wavelength law.
   The redshift law $1+z=(c_0/c_z)^{3/2}$ *is* momentum conservation plus the
   ruler's $c^{-3/2}$ scaling. (Already derived; newly framed.)
2. **Free massive particles (new check):** $L=\tfrac12m(t)v^2$ gives conserved
   canonical momentum $p=m(t)v$ (the $m(t)$ time dependence breaks energy, not
   momentum), so $v\propto c^{-1/2}$; against the local velocity unit
   ($\propto c$): $\hat v\propto c^{-3/2}$, i.e.
   $$\hat v_\text{pec}(z_\text{obs}{=}0)=\frac{\hat v_\text{pec}(z)}{1+z},$$
   **exactly the standard peculiar-velocity decay** $v_\text{pec}\propto1/a$ of
   free-streaming matter in an expanding universe.
3. **Decoupled gas temperature (new check):** $\hat T_\text{gas}\propto\hat v^2
   \propto(1+z)^2$, versus the photon sector's $\hat T_\gamma\propto(1+z)$ — the
   standard pair of adiabatic cooling laws ($a^{-2}$ vs $a^{-1}$), reproduced with
   no new assumptions.

Rows 2 and 3 extend the correspondence table (photometry, thermal sector, now
free-streaming kinematics); together with row 1 they support a single sentence the
merged Foundation should own: *in this framework nothing propagating ever loses
momentum — observers' units outgrow it.*
