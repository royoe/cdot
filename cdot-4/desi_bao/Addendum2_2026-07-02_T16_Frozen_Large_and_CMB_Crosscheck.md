# Addendum 2 to the T16 BAO Update — The "Frozen-Large" Picture Was the One Tested; a New CMB Cross-Probe Check (2026-07-02)

*Session type: constructive (clarification + one new adverse check). The author
clarifies the intended interpretation: the BAO structures were imprinted **large to
start with** — at essentially the size observed today — and frozen ever since; the
"small proper ruler stretched by expansion" reading was never the model's picture.
This addendum records: (1) that the frozen-large picture is **exactly what the DESI
test assumed** — the $r_d$ marginalization is that picture; (2) the failure restated
inside that picture, where it becomes maximally intuitive: the same frozen object
would have to be up to 10% longer across the sky than along the line of sight; and
(3) a new cross-probe check the frozen-large framing invites, which the model also
fails: the CMB acoustic angle and the galaxy BAO demand frozen scales of 184 Mpc and
122 Mpc respectively — a 51% split where ΛCDM uses one number.*

---

## 1. The Clarification: No Interpretive Mismatch in the Fit

The previous addendum's §3 reductio (a 0.134 Mpc proper ruler) addressed only the
literal reading of "energy scaling makes the initial conditions identical to the
standard model's" — identical *local proper* physics at recombination. The author's
actual picture — **structures imprinted at their present size and frozen** — is
different, and it is precisely what the test implemented: leaving $r_d$ free and
letting the fit choose the frozen size is the frozen-large hypothesis with the size
as a parameter. The fit chose 121.7 Mpc. There was no misunderstanding in either
direction: the author's picture was the tested picture.

## 2. The Failure, Restated Inside the Frozen-Large Picture

In the frozen-large picture the verdict reads: **the same frozen structure would have
to be up to 10% longer across the sky than along the line of sight, with the
anisotropy varying systematically in redshift.** A single frozen isotropic object
cannot do this at any size — the size is a free knob the fit already turned. The
failure is *directional*, not dimensional; imprint size, imprint mechanism, and
imprint epoch cannot touch it.

## 3. New Check: the CMB Acoustic Angle vs the Galaxy BAO (adverse)

The frozen-large picture invites a cross-probe consistency test the main update did
not use. In any acoustic story, the CMB peak structure and the matter BAO are the
*same* imprint, so the same frozen $L$ must serve both:

- **Galaxy BAO** (this update's fit): $L = 121.7$ Mpc.
- **CMB acoustic angle:** the last-scattering surface sits at
  $D_p(z{=}1100) = R_0[1-(1101)^{-1/6}] = 17{,}702$ Mpc; the observed acoustic scale
  $\theta_* = 1.0411\times10^{-2}$ rad then requires
  $L_\text{CMB} = \theta_* D_p = \mathbf{184\ Mpc}$.

$L_\text{CMB}/L_\text{BAO} = 1.51$ — **a 51% cross-probe split**, on top of the 10%
radial/transverse split, where ΛCDM serves both probes with the single
$r_d = 147$ Mpc because its mapping stretches the two consistently. Stated
assumption: the model's CMB feature is viewed geometrically at the last-scattering
distance ($\theta = L/D_p$, straight lines in the static map) — the same optics as
the transverse galaxy BAO, so the two cannot be split by propagation physics. This
check also stress-tests T16's existing "first acoustic peak translates" claim, which
should be re-examined against whichever distance mapping it implicitly used; on the
model's own mapping as audited here, the first-peak position does *not* come out
consistent with the matter BAO.
