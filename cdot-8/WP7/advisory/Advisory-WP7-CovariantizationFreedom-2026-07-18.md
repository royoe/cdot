## 2. What survives: the two-anchor pinning

The catch removes my derivation of the crossover shape; it does not
remove the asymptotics, because those were never the convention's to
give — they belong to two physical requirements already established:

- **Anchor 1 — separate-universe continuity at $k\to0$** (the exact
  check §6 installed as the program's own): a super-horizon mode is
  locally a shifted background, so the field-side term must vanish
  smoothly as $k\to0$. The no-cancellation reading (one strictly global
  constraint, measure-zero per-mode force) makes the term's ratio to
  gradient terms $(aH/k)^2$ — **divergent** as $k\to0$, discontinuous
  against the exact $k=0$ cancellation: a super-horizon mode would feel
  an M5 force its own shifted-background physics doesn't contain.
  Excluded.
- **Anchor 2 — WP5's local decoupling at $kR_h\gg1$**: the cancellation
  must die off sub-horizon (local physics keeps the full
  sliding-condensate term, $(aH/k)^2$-suppressed — the PPN sector's own
  established structure). The pointwise/full-enslavement reading
  violates this from the other side. Excluded.

**Any admissible covariantization therefore interpolates: cancellation
factor $\to1$ at $k\to0$, $\to0$ at $kR_h\gg1$.** The windowed form
$-F_Q(1-\mathcal W(kR_h))$ satisfies both by construction — with
$\mathcal W$ now an *interpolating function with pinned asymptotes*, not
a derived top-hat. The low-$\ell$ M5 structure — both halves, Einstein-
side and field-side — exists with pinned asymptotics regardless of the
convention; its detailed shape at $kR_h\sim1$ does not.
