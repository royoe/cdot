## Observational Background

### The M-σ Relation

Unaffected. The M-σ relation (Ferrarese & Merritt 2000; Gebhardt et al. 2000) states
$M_\text{BH}\propto\sigma^4$, correlating the central supermassive black hole mass with
the host *bulge's* velocity dispersion, not the disk. The same relation connects to the
BTFR through $M_\text{BH}\propto v_f^4=GM_\text{bary}\,g_\dagger$ (T14) — a derived
consequence, not an independent coincidence, unaffected in this structural claim by the
counting-law change (only $g_\dagger$'s numerical value is open, as throughout T6/T14/T15).

For the disk ($f\to1$), the BTFR gives $v_f^4=GM_\text{bary}\,g_\dagger$ directly. For
the bulge (coherence factor $f\approx0.627\,v_\text{rot}/\sigma$, below), the effective
scale is $g_\dagger^\text{eff}=f^2g_\dagger=(\pi/8)\beta^2g_\dagger$,
$\beta=v_\text{rot}/\sigma$:
$$\boxed{M_\text{bulge}\sim\frac{8}{\pi\beta^2}\,\frac{\sigma^4}{G\,g_\dagger}.}$$
The exponent 4 is preserved; the prefactor $8/(\pi\beta^2)$ varies with rotational
support ($\beta\sim0.3$–$1.0$ → prefactor $\sim28$–$2.5$), explaining the disk/bulge
normalization offset and predicting M-σ scatter tracks $v_\text{rot}/\sigma$. **All of
this is unaffected by the counting-law change** — it is a statement about the
*functional form*, with $g_\dagger$ entering as an external parameter throughout,
exactly the same separability T14/T15 already established for the RAR closure itself.

**Key observational facts, unchanged:** M-σ correlates with the spheroid, not the disk;
giant ellipticals and BCGs show over-massive BHs (the relation steepens at the top
end); ellipticals are pressure-supported with no disk.

### The Disk-vs-Elliptical Dichotomy

Unaffected. Spirals are rotation-dominated; ellipticals are pressure-dominated. The
most massive SMBHs are found in giant ellipticals.

### What Sources $B_c$

Unaffected. $B_c$ is sourced by the baryonic mass distribution coupled to $g_\dagger$ —
not by the central black hole, which would break the RAR's universality. The black hole
mass *correlates* with $B_c$ (both track $GM_\text{bary}\,g_\dagger$) but is a
correlate, not a cause.

**$B_c$ is a vector: amplitude from the sea, axis from bulk circulation.** Unaffected:
an isotropic connecton sea supplies amplitude but no preferred direction; the axis is
set by the galaxy's coherent mass current.
- **Disk** (ordered rotation): $f\to1$, Lorentz filter at full strength.
- **Bulge** (dispersion-dominated): $f\approx\sqrt{\pi/8}\,(v_\text{rot}/\sigma)\approx
  0.627\,v_\text{rot}/\sigma$ (Monte-Carlo of a drifting Maxwellian), effective
  $B_c\to fB_c$, $g_\dagger^\text{eff}=f^2g_\dagger$ for the bulge.

None of this derivation references the cosmological counting law.

### Ordered vs. Random Orbits: Disk Stripping

Unaffected. The Lorentz term is large for ordered (rotation-dominated) velocity, small
for random (pressure-dominated) velocity — disk stars are selectively stripped,
spheroid stars retained. A galaxy high on the shared $GM_\text{bary}\,g_\dagger$
normalization has both a stronger $B_c$ and a more massive SMBH; both are downstream
of the same baryonic normalization, not causally linked to each other.
