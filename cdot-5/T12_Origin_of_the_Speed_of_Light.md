# T12 — The Origin of the Speed of Light

*This document needed real rework, not just verification — it is premise 2's own home
document, and premise 2 is exactly what changed (Core Principles §1). Checked
section by section: the foundational relational principle survives; the specific
"horizon counting" mechanics, the count-vs-mass fork, and the Sciama-relation
computation are all rewritten below; and one new gap is found and flagged — the
connecton-ontology section's holographic-saturation formula leans on the same

## Physical Concept

In standard physics, the speed of light $c$ is a fundamental constant — a fixed
parameter of the laws of nature with no explanation for why it takes the value it
does. This model proposes that $c$ is not a constant but a **dynamical, relational
quantity**: its value is set by the matter content of the universe.

This is the model's deepest organizing principle. Understanding it means understanding
why the model is genuinely Machian in character, what "Machian" means here, and what
remains open.

---

## The Relational Principle

The foundational statement, unchanged from cdot-4:

> **The global matter content of the universe sets the speed of light $c$; and $c$
> sets every energy, length, time, and coupling. Therefore all physical scales are
> referred to the cosmic matter content.**

This is **counting-Machianism** — the model is Machian about $c$, not about inertial
mass. This distinguishes it from:

- **Sciama's Machianism** (potential-Machianism): inertial mass is generated by the
  gravitational potential of all other matter ($GM_u/(R_uc^2)\approx1$, Sciama 1953).
  The model explicitly rejects this — see below for what changed in *how* this
  rejection is demonstrated.
- **Brans-Dicke theory**: the gravitational coupling $G$ is a field sourced by
  matter. Related but not the same.
- **Standard VSL theories** (Barrow-Magueijo): $c$ varies but is not tied to a specific
  physical mechanism.

**What changed for cdot-5:** the *mechanism* behind "counting" changed. cdot-4 counted
**occupancy** — particles physically present within a horizon of growing volume.
cdot-5 counts **connectivity** — how much of the connecton network a local point
currently *reaches*. Both are still "the global matter content sets $c$"; they differ
in what, precisely, "counting" means.

## Premise 2: Connectivity Counting

*Rewritten in full — cdot-4's version of this section described occupancy counting
verbatim (a static, uniform particle sea; $N=n\cdot\tfrac43\pi R^3\propto R^3$) as if it
were the only possible reading of premise 2. It no longer is.*

The causal horizon still grows at rate $dR/dt=c(t)$ (unchanged — this is a statement
about light propagation through static space, Core Principles §3, independent of what
determines $c$). What changed is what $c$ tracks. Rather than the number of particles
*occupying* the growing causal volume, premise 2 now states that $c$ tracks the
connecton network's **reach** from the local point — how many connectons are
transitively connected to it, not how many exist within a given spatial region.

As the horizon widens, it admits a new shell of the network into potential contact,
but a node in that shell only joins the *locally reachable* set if it links to a node
already in that set — so the reach's growth rate is proportional to the reach already
held, not to the raw shell volume:
$$\frac{dN}{dR}=\frac NL\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\propto e^{R/L}, \qquad c\propto N.$$
$L$ is a fixed length (the network's recruitment length) — this is the entire content
of the new premise 2, and the "fixed length, not fixed fraction of the horizon" contrast
with occupancy counting is what makes the law exponential rather than a power law (Core
Principles §1).

**The relational content, restated for connectivity:** in a universe with no network to
connect to, reach is zero and $c=0$ — all kinematics become trivial (no propagation, no
clocks). Matter does not merely *exist in* a universe with a given $c$; the matter (via
the network it supports) *creates* the kinematic arena. This is the same relational
statement cdot-4 made; only the noun changed, from "particles present" to "connections
reached."

## The Premise Fork: Retired

*cdot-4 devoted a major section to a fork between "particle count" ($c\propto N$,
weighting every quantum including relativistic species) and "classical Mach"
($c\propto M_u$, weighting only rest mass) — two readings of occupancy counting that
agreed today but diverged in the radiation era and the far future. **This entire fork
is now moot, and should not be treated as a menu of remaining options for
connectivity counting.***

The reason is a finding from T23 (the BAO investigation that selected connectivity
counting in the first place): under *either* occupancy reading — count or mass — the
horizon's counted content is still $\propto R^n$ for uniform density (only the
effective $n$ or the normalization constant differs between weighting every quantum
vs. only rest mass). Both readings are members of the *same* excluded power-law family
that DESI's Alcock-Paczyński test rules out for every exponent (T23 §1.6, reconfirmed by
direct reverse-engineering: a power law in proper time is *algebraically the same
family* as a power law in coordinate time, just relabeled — neither escapes). So the
fork was never a live escape route once the exponential law was on the table; it
diverged over *whose weight counts*, not over *whether occupancy itself is the right
concept*, and occupancy itself is what connectivity counting replaces.

**A new, analogous question is open instead**, not previously posed because it didn't
arise under occupancy counting: does the connecton network's *reach* depend on which
species anchor the nodes being reached (e.g., does reach toward baryon-anchored
regions differ from reach toward regions with no massive anchor at all), the way the old
fork asked whether relativistic species count toward $N$? This is genuinely
unaddressed — flagged as an open question below, not answered here, and it should not
be confused with the old, now-closed fork.

**What survives from the old fork's discussion, unaffected:** the neutrino-runaway
check (integrating stellar neutrino production over the stellar era gives only
$\sim10^{-5}$ of the relic neutrino sea — no stellar-burning instability) was a
check on occupancy counting specifically and is retired along with the fork it was
checking; it does not obviously translate to a connectivity-counting analogue and has
not been re-examined.

## The Sciama Relation: Why It Fails Here — Rebuilt, With an Honest Limit

cdot-4 demonstrated the Sciama relation's failure by computing
$GM_u/(R_uc^2)\propto R^2/c^2$ and substituting the volume law's $R\propto c^{1/3}$ to
get a clean power law, $\propto c^{-4/3}$. **That substitution step no longer works, and
not just because the exponent changes — the construction itself breaks down.**

Under connectivity counting, $R$ relates to $c$ **logarithmically**, not by a power
law: from $c(R)=c_0e^{(R-R_\text{now})/L}$,
$$R-R_\text{now}=L\ln(c/c_0).$$
Taking the natural convention $R_\text{now}=0$ (Core Principles §3: only differences
$R-R_\text{now}$ enter anything physical, so this is a free, harmless choice), and using
the redshift law $c/c_0=(1+z)^{-1/2}$:
$$R(z)=-\frac L2\ln(1+z),$$
which is **negative for every $z>0$** — every epoch before today has $R<0$ in this
coordinate.

The old Sciama computation needed $M_u\propto R^3$ (uniform matter density times
enclosed volume, treating $R$ as a literal radius). **Applying that same formula here
gives a negative enclosed mass for all of cosmic history except this instant** — an
immediate absurdity, not a subtle asymptotic one. This is not merely "the coefficient
changed" (as with T6's $g_\dagger$); the construction itself does not survive, because
$R$ under connectivity counting is a bookkeeping coordinate that can go negative, and
"mass enclosed within a negative radius" is not a physical quantity.

**What this means, and what it doesn't.** The Sciama relation's *qualitative* failure —
that the model's Machianism lives in the counting rule, not in a gravitational-potential
relation — is not threatened; if anything, a counting law with even less structural
resemblance to "a horizon of a given size enclosing a given mass" has *less* room for a
Sciama-type coincidence to hide in, not more. But the specific, clean *quantitative*
demonstration cdot-4 gave ($\propto c^{-4/3}$) has no direct replacement, for the same
underlying reason T6's $g_\dagger=c^2/R_0$ derivation does not survive: both leaned on
treating the counted horizon as if it had a well-defined physical size $R$, which
connectivity counting does not supply. This is the same gap recurring in a second
place, not a new, independent one — worth remembering as a pattern (see Open Questions).

## Physical Content: What Does "$c\to0$ for a Lone Particle" Mean?

Restated for connectivity counting; the conclusion is unchanged, the mechanism
description updates from occupancy language to reach language. If a point has no
connections to the rest of the network — the connectivity analogue of "$N=0$" — then
$c\to0$. This does not mean the particle has zero inertia or zero mass — its rest mass
is invariant (premise 3). Rather, it means:
- No electromagnetic signals can propagate.
- No clocks exist (atomic frequencies $\nu\propto c^2\to0$).
- No rulers exist (Bohr radius $\propto c^{-1}\to\infty$).
- No gravitational interaction is meaningful ($G$ is constant but there is no other
  mass to interact with, and no signals to propagate).

Physics becomes trivial in the sense that no observational comparison can be made. The
particle exists, but nothing happens. This is arguably a cleaner Machian statement than
Sciama's: instead of saying inertia vanishes (which is hard to test), the model says the
kinematic arena vanishes. It is the difference between "the particle cannot move" and
"there is nothing to move relative to or measure with." **Note the language shift from
cdot-4**: "no particles nearby" (occupancy) becomes "no connections reached"
(connectivity) — a lone particle with literally nothing to link to is the connectivity
analogue of an empty horizon, and the physical conclusion carries over unchanged.

## What Is a Connecton? Neither Photon Nor Graviton

*This section's core argument (connectons cannot obey $E=\hbar\omega$; the alternative
reading as a conserved unit of relation rather than a field quantum) is about the
connecton's *category*, not about the cosmological counting law, and survives
unchanged. But one piece of its supporting derivation — the holographic saturation
count — inherits T6's $R_0$ gap, flagged explicitly below rather than silently carried
forward.*

T14 introduces the connecton as the physical carrier of the relational principle above,
but never pins down what *kind* of thing it is. Since it must propagate at $c$, the
natural first guess is that it is photon-like or graviton-like — the only two massless
species physics already has. This section argues that guess is wrong, on the model's
own terms, and proposes a coherent alternative.

Masslessness plus motion at $c$ does not imply "photon or graviton." Physics already
has a third, well-precedented category: new, weakly-coupled massless or near-massless
bosons that are neither (quintessence fields, dilatons, "dark photons," axion-like
particles in their massless limit). Nothing about $E=pc$ forces a species to be one of
the two forces we happen to have quantized.

### A sharper, model-internal argument: connectons cannot obey $E=\hbar\omega$

T14's own results go further than the general point and **exclude** both
identifications quantitatively. T14 derives a conserved-number cascade
$n(k)\propto1/k$ (constant number per $\ln k$) spanning roughly
$\ln(R_0/\lambda_\text{Compton})\approx89$ e-folds, and separately fixes the *total* sea
count via holographic saturation,
$$n_\text{holo}=\frac{3}{4L_p^2R_0},$$
**with every unit contributing a single fixed energy scale** ($m_cc^2=\hbar H^\text{hor}$,
giving $\rho_\text{bg}=(\pi/6)\rho_\text{crit}$). These two results are only
consistent with each other if the connecton's energy is the same at every point in the
cascade. But if a connecton were a standard field quantum, mode $k$ would carry
$E=\hbar ck$, and the flat-per-$\ln k$ number density would then give wildly excessive
energy density at any wavelength shorter than the horizon. A connecton cannot
simultaneously have a fixed, position-independent energy $\sim\hbar H_0$ and obey
$E=\hbar\omega$ across an 89-e-fold spectrum — the two T14 results are incompatible
under the standard dispersion relation. **This argument's logic survives connectivity
counting untouched** — it is about the dispersion relation, not about how $c(t)$'s
cosmological history is generated.

**The flagged gap:** both $n_\text{holo}=3/(4L_p^2R_0)$ and the candidate energy-scale
value $\hbar c/R_0=1/6\approx0.167$ (in units of $\hbar H_0$, listed among T14's
candidate normalizations) use $R_0$ as **the present horizon size** — precisely the
construct T6 already found has no connectivity-counting analogue (Core Principles §3:
$R$ is a bookkeeping coordinate, not a physical size; naively substituting $L$ for
$R_0$ in T6's $g_\dagger$ formula missed the observed MOND scale by $\sim2.8\times$).
Whether the same naive substitution here (replacing $R_0\to L$ in the holographic count
and the candidate energy scale) gives anything physically sensible has **not been
checked** — flagged as a new open item, inherited from the same root cause as T6's,
not a fresh problem.

### The alternative: a unit of connection, not a particle

Unchanged from cdot-4 — this reading does not depend on the $R_0$ value flagged above,
only on the *category* argument. The coherent reading is that a connecton is not a
field quantum but a **conserved unit of relation** between a local degree of freedom
and the cosmological horizon — closer to a bit of holographic/entanglement structure
than to a particle:

- Its fixed energy scale ($\sim\hbar H_0$) is a thermodynamic bit cost (a Landauer-type
  cost of one unit of horizon information), not a de Broglie energy. Candidate values
  in units of $\hbar H_0$: Landauer, $\ln2/2\pi\approx0.110$; T14's existing
  $\hbar c/R_0=1/6\approx0.167$ (inherits the $R_0$ gap above); T14's existing
  $m_c=\hbar H^\text{hor}/c^2=0.5$ (does not reference $R_0$, unaffected). Not yet
  resolved even in cdot-4; now compounded by the $R_0$ question for one of the three
  candidates.
- "Wavelength" in the cascade is a correlation/link length, not a de Broglie
  wavelength — resolves the dispersion tension by removing the premise that forced it.
- "Propagates at $c$" describes how disturbances along the connection move — $c$ is
  the network's causal update speed, not the translational speed of a traveling
  particle.
- Holographic saturation is T14's own application of the standard "one bit per
  $4L_p^2$" holographic entropy count (Bekenstein-Hawking); this supports reading the
  connecton as a generic holographic degree of freedom, though it is a consistency
  point rather than an independent confirmation (an earlier draft's stronger claim, "the
  exact identity that clinches it," was found to be a tautology and was corrected in
  cdot-4 — recorded in `cdot-4/T23_The_Failed_Tests.md` §2.5).
- Photons and gravitons are then derived, collective excitations of the same
  substrate — EM as the network's polarizability, gravity as $\delta n,w$'s
  coarse-grained state.

## Open Questions

- **A derivation of the connectivity law from more fundamental principles.** cdot-4
  asked this of $c\propto N$ (occupancy); the analogous question now is whether there
  is a variational, information-theoretic, or holographic argument that *forces*
  reach to grow autocatalytically ($dN/dR=N/L$) rather than this being three named,
  undecided assumptions (network supercriticality; the endpoint-only $1/L$ recruitment
  rate; mean-field independence — Core Principles §7).
- **What sets $L$?** cdot-4 asked what sets $k$ in $c=kR^3$; the direct analogue.
  Currently one free scale, degenerate with the sound-horizon normalization. Candidates
  on record: a re-anchoring mean free path (T14's diffusive fraction), the Compton
  length of the lightest massive species. Not the horizon itself (that would be
  scale-free and give back the excluded power-law family).
- **New: does the holographic saturation count and its candidate energy scale
  ($n_\text{holo}$, $\hbar c/R_0$) survive under $R_0\to L$?** Flagged above; not
  checked. This is the same open question as T6's $g_\dagger$ re-derivation, arising a
  second time in a different part of the theory — worth resolving once, in T14, in a
  way that fixes both rather than patching each site separately.
- **New: is there a connectivity-counting analogue of the old count-vs-mass fork** —
  does network reach depend on which species anchor the nodes reached? Genuinely
  unaddressed, not merely retired along with the old fork (see "The Premise Fork"
  above).
- **Retired, not carried forward:** the BBN discriminator between count and mass
  (cdot-4's leading candidate observational test of the old fork) is moot along with
  the fork itself — there is no "mass reading" of connectivity counting to discriminate
  against. T13's own BBN treatment should be checked independently for whether *any*
  cdot-5-specific BBN question survives (the $c(t)$ history at BBN epochs did change),
  but not this particular one.
